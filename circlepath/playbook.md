# CirclePath demonstration playbook

This is an editable demonstration preparation resource, not an approved program curriculum.
The program manager must replace or approve it for their own practice before real use.

## Every role
- Keep participants' choices and confidentiality in view throughout the process.
- Describe the source of information. A referral report and a participant's account can differ.
- Invite questions and passing. Do not presume readiness from attendance or a completed checkbox.
- Refer questions about reporting duties, safety, or program eligibility to the designated practitioner.

## Facilitator and co-facilitator
- Prepare separately with the people involved as appropriate for the chosen process.
- Confirm ongoing willingness, accessible arrangements, and the needs of the harmed party and young person.
- Use open questions: What happened from your perspective? Who was affected? What support is needed?
- Ask what meaningful repair could look like without prescribing an apology or forgiveness.
- Review the process with participants and pause when a person requests it.

## Community members
- Speak from your own perspective. Ask permission before offering personal experiences.
- Avoid generalizations about young people, diagnostic labels, moral lectures, and predictions about outcomes.
- Attend to impact, needs, responsibilities, and relationships.

## Support liaison
- Confirm practical needs such as transport, interpretation, and accessibility with people directly.
- Follow up on agreed commitments with support-oriented questions, not assumptions about non-compliance.
- Bring barriers and requested agreement changes to the facilitator or manager.

## Documentation
- Separate what was reported, what was directly observed, and what participants explicitly agreed.
- Preserve attribution where accounts differ. Use only the minimum information needed for the purpose.
- Private debriefs stay outside the AI context and shared learning library.
- Completion of a task does not prove healing, remorse, or safety.

Reference for program review: Colorado Restorative Justice Practitioner Guidelines, June 2024,
https://rjcolorado.org/restorative-justice-defined/standards/
